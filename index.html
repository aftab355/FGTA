Bradley–Terry ratings Maximum likelihood
A proper statistical rating model fit over every game at once, rather than one-at-a-time like Elo. It finds the set of strengths that makes the actual results most probable — so it doesn't care what order games were played in, and it's far more honest at small samples. Shown beside Elo so you can see where they disagree.
Rating confidence Bootstrap
Resamples the whole season hundreds of times and recomputes every rating, producing a 90% band around each one. A wide band means "we don't really know yet" — the honest answer the single Elo number hides.
True skill Bayesian
Elo gives one number. This gives a distribution — a skill estimate plus how uncertain it is, using Glicko. Few games = wide band = the ladder doesn't really know yet.
Is X actually better than Y?
Probability each row player's true skill exceeds each column player's, accounting for uncertainty.
Dominance network PageRank
Treats every result as a vote — losers point to winners — and runs the PageRank algorithm over the whole web of results. It rewards beating players who themselves beat strong players, capturing transitive dominance that head-to-head records and Elo both miss.
Rating trajectory Kalman filter
Tracks each player's rating as a moving target: a Kalman filter estimates both the current smoothed rating and its momentum (points per game) from the noisy sequence of post-match Elo ratings, then projects 3 games ahead if the current trend holds. Wide bands mean the trend is uncertain, not that the model failed.

Point-level match model Barnett–Clarke
Feeds each player's own serve-point win rate into the exact hierarchical formulas — point → game → set → match — used in academic tennis modeling. No simulation, closed-form recursion. Only works for matches logged through the Point Tracker, since plain set scores don't carry a serve/return split (service is assumed to alternate starting with player 1).
Dominance Ratio & TPW% Sackmann
DR = % return points won ÷ (1 − % serve points won). Above 1.0 means a player wins more of their return points than their opponents win off them — the standard measure of who's actually dominating point-for-point, independent of who happened to close out the close games.
Recency-weighted rating Form, not history
The headline Elo weighs a match from a year ago the same as one from yesterday. This re-scores every result as a performance rating (opponent's pre-match rating ±400 for a win/loss) and exponentially decays older results with a 45-day half-life — it answers "who's actually good right now", not "who's been good ever".
Glicko-2 + volatility
"True skill" above uses rating + deviation (Glicko-1). This runs the full Glickman Glicko-2 algorithm, adding a third number: volatility (σ) — how erratic a player's results are game to game. A high-σ player is a coin flip on any given night even if their rating is solid; a low-σ player is exactly as good as their number says.
Workload ACWR
Sports-science acute:chronic workload ratio — matches in the last 7 days ÷ the last 28 days' weekly average. Above 1.5 is the injury/burnout risk zone; under 0.8 means barely playing. It flags who's grinding too much or too little, not just who's winning.

Model calibration Validation
Every prediction the ladder has ever made, checked against what actually happened. If the model says 70% and those games are won 70% of the time, it's honest. Brier score measures accuracy — lower is better, and 0.250 is what a coin flip scores.
Is momentum real? Significance
Streaks feel meaningful, but random sequences produce streaks too. This runs a Wald–Wolfowitz runs test and compares form after wins versus after losses.
Leverage index Stakes
Which unplayed matchup would shake the table hardest? Scores every pairing by how much the standings move depending on who wins.
Alternate timelines Luck vs skill
Replays your exact schedule — same fixtures, same order — thousands of times, letting each result fall by its true probability. Where you land in this universe vs all the others tells you how much was skill and how much was luck.
Optimal path to #1 Beam search
Searches thousands of possible match sequences to find the genuinely fastest route to the top — which opponents, in which order. Usually beats simply beating whoever's directly above you.
Butterfly effect Counterfactual
Flip one past result and replay every game after it. Because ratings feed forward, one changed night can rewrite the whole season.
